# Blackwhite

Blackwhite is a Newspeak word defined in Emmanuel Goldstein's forbidden treatise The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism as the ability to assert that black is white whenever the Party requires it, and it is one of the three mental disciplines — crimestop, blackwhite and doublethink — around which a Party member's training is built. It names the routine falsification of reality on which the Party's claim to infallibility depends, and because its definition is itself built on two contradictory meanings, the word already performs the doublethink it exists to describe.

## Two Meanings in One Word

As the book describes it, blackwhite has two mutually contradictory meanings. Applied to an opponent, it means the habit of impudently claiming that black is white in contradiction of the plain facts — the accusation the Party directs at heretics, saboteurs and traitors. Applied to a Party member, it means a loyal willingness to say that black is white when Party discipline demands this. But the second meaning goes well beyond outward compliance: it includes the ability to believe that black is white, to know that black is white, and to forget that one has ever believed the contrary. The word therefore names not merely a lie but the successful erasure of the memory of any earlier truth.

Because the word is defined as having both meanings at once, understanding it as the Party understands it already involves the mental contradiction it describes. This is not sloppy definition but deliberate design: blackwhite is the point where the vocabulary of Newspeak ceases to record reality and instead trains the mind to hold opposites together.

## A Keyword of Mental Training

The treatise presents blackwhite as the key to an orthodoxy far more demanding than mere stupidity. Oceanic society rests on the conviction that Big Brother is omnipotent and the Party infallible, but since in reality Big Brother is not omnipotent and the Party is not infallible, there is need for an unwearying, moment-to-moment flexibility in the treatment of facts. The word supplies that flexibility. Full orthodoxy is compared to the control of a contortionist over his body: every perception, every statement, every memory must be bendable to the needs of the moment.

The word belongs to an elaborate mental training undergone in childhood and grouping itself round crimestop, blackwhite and doublethink, a training that makes the Party member unwilling and unable to think too deeply on any subject. Crimestop is described as protective stupidity, the faculty of stopping short at the threshold of a dangerous thought; blackwhite is the positive side of the same discipline, the power to affirm the required falsehood; doublethink is the system that holds both together. The goal is the goodthinker, the naturally orthodox person who knows, without taking thought, what the true belief or the desirable emotion is.

## Blackwhite and the Rewriting of the Past

According to the book, blackwhite demands a continuous alteration of the past, and that alteration is made possible by doublethink, the system of thought which really embraces all the rest. The link is causal: before the Party can declare that an event never happened, the individual mind must be trained to hold the old and the new versions simultaneously and then to forget that any change was made. The Ministry of Truth exists to perform the documentary half of the work — denying memories, rewriting records, annihilating the past on a daily basis — while blackwhite is the subjective half, the willingness of the Party member to keep step with those corrections.

Pynchon's introduction links this fictional mechanism to what Orwell called the 'sort of schizophrenic manner of thinking' of the English Left in 1948, in which words like 'democracy' could bear two irreconcilable meanings and such things as concentration camps and mass deportations could be right and wrong simultaneously. Blackwhite is the novel's formal name for that schizophrenia. It converts a historical political hypocrisy into a permanent structure of the mind, making the falsification of history not an occasional deception but a continuous condition of life.

## Blackwhite in Action: The Interrogation

The novel stages blackwhite at its most naked when O'Brien holds up four fingers and demands that Winston count five. Under torture Winston finally screams "Five! Five! Five!", and O'Brien at once replies, "No, Winston, that is no use. You are lying. You still think there are four." The object is not a verbal surrender but a perceptual one; later, with the dial still exerting its pressure, Winston really does see five fingers, "and there was no deformity," before the familiar world floods back. Blackwhite is thus not hypocrisy but conversion: the final stage is not saying the lie but seeing the lie as truth.

## Enforcement and the Absence of Law

Blackwhite is described as a mental technique, but it operates in a legal vacuum backed by terror. In Oceania there were no longer any laws, so that nothing was illegal, yet merely opening a diary could be punished by death or at least twenty-five years in a forced-labour camp. A Party member lives from birth to death under the eye of the Thought Police, where every eccentricity, change of habit and nervous mannerism is read as a symptom of inner struggle, and where purges and vaporisations are not punishments for committed crimes but the wiping-out of persons who might commit a crime in the future. Blackwhite is the internal complement to this external machinery: because the Party never admits error, the individual must be ready at every moment to see the world as the Party declares it to be.

The Ministry of Love stands behind the word. It is the really frightening Ministry: a windowless building impossible to enter except on official business, ringed by barbed-wire entanglements, steel doors and hidden machine-gun nests, with gorilla-faced guards in black uniforms patrolling the streets that lead to it. The serene, almost technical sound of 'blackwhite' is thus paired in the structure of Oceania with the place where minds are broken; the word names the desired outcome, and the Ministry supplies the pressure that produces it.

## Significance in Orwell's Political Critique

Orwell's own politics — 'to the left of Left', formed in Spain against Franco's fascists and against false anti-fascism — give the word its diagnostic edge. In March 1948 he complained that nearly the whole of the English Left had been driven to accept the Russian regime as 'Socialist' while silently recognizing that its spirit and practice were alien to anything meant by socialism in England, producing the schizophrenic manner of thinking that blackwhite codifies. By compressing that contemporary hypocrisy into a single Newspeak term, Orwell turned an intellectual failure of his own generation into a general warning about the cost of ideological conformity, and the word remains one of the novel's most enduring contributions to political language.
