# Comrade Ogilvy

Comrade Ogilvy is a wholly fabricated Party member, created on the spot by Winston Smith during his work at the Ministry of Truth. With the task of rewriting Big Brother's Order for the Day after the original subject, Comrade Withers, had been vaporized, Winston needed a piece of pure fantasy. He conjured up a hero who had never existed—a humble, rank-and-file Party member whose life and death could be held up as an example to be followed. The decision to invent Ogilvy was a casual act of forgery, but it illustrates the ease with which the Party manufactures history: a few lines of print and a couple of faked photographs would bring him into existence.

## Fabricated Biography and Achievements

According to the narrative Winston dictated in Big Brother's style, Comrade Ogilvy's life was a parade of model orthodoxy from infancy. At age three he refused all toys except a drum, a sub-machine gun and a model helicopter. At six—a year early, by special relaxation of the rules—he joined the Spies; at nine he became a troop leader. At eleven he denounced his own uncle to the Thought Police after overhearing a conversation that seemed to him to have criminal tendencies. At seventeen he was a district organiser of the Junior Anti-Sex League. At nineteen he designed a hand grenade adopted by the Ministry of Peace, which killed thirty-one Eurasian prisoners in a single trial. At twenty-three he perished in action: pursued by enemy jet planes while flying over the Indian Ocean with important despatches, he weighted his body with his machine-gun and leapt from the helicopter into deep water, despatches and all. Big Brother added that this end was impossible to contemplate without feelings of envy.

## Narrative Role and Thematic Significance

Ogilvy's existence—or rather his non-existence—underscores the Party's absolute control over the past and the triviality with which truth can be altered. He is a product of the same system that produces statistics, victories and enemies on demand. Winston, who knows Ogilvy is a lie, nevertheless feels a "profound conviction" that his version will be adopted over that of his rival Tillotson, because the lie has already become a fact in the Ministry's records. Ogilvy represents the ideal Party member: celibate, abstemious, fanatically loyal, and devoid of any personal life beyond the service of Ingsoc. His creation also reveals the psychological cost of Winston's job—the constant, wearying effort to fabricate realities that must then be treated as true. Though Ogilvy never existed, his file will be preserved, and future historians will find him as authentic as Charlemagne or Julius Caesar.
