# Death-Worship

Death-Worship, also rendered as Obliteration of the Self, is the ruling ideology of the superstate Eastasia in the world of Nineteen Eighty-Four. It is one of three parallel totalitarian systems—the others being Ingsoc in Oceania and Neo-Bolshevism in Eurasia—that are presented as mutually hostile yet structurally identical. The name itself is a translation of a Chinese term, and Oceania's propaganda portrays it as a barbarous outrage against morality and common sense, while deliberately keeping its actual tenets unknown to the average citizen.

## Origins and Definition

Death-Worship emerged in the middle years of the twentieth century as one of the new political movements that consciously aimed to perpetuate unfreedom and inequality. According to the book-within-the-novel, The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism, these movements grew out of earlier socialist currents but abandoned the goal of liberty and equality. In Eastasia, the philosophy is sometimes called Obliteration of the Self, a name that suggests the complete subordination of individual identity to the collective—a principle that, in practice, differs little from the demands of Ingsoc or Neo-Bolshevism.

## Comparison with Other Ideologies

Although the citizen of Oceania is forbidden any knowledge of Death-Worship's doctrines and is taught to despise them, the three superstates' social systems are in fact indistinguishable. All exhibit the same pyramidal hierarchy, the same veneration of a semi-divine leader, and the same economy built on and for continuous warfare. The novel emphasizes that the three philosophies prop one another up: their conflict is necessary to maintain the illusion of ideological difference, while in reality each regime operates on identical principles of control, surveillance, and the denial of individual autonomy.

## Narrative Significance

Death-Worship functions in the novel as an emblem of the totalitarian world's uniformity. It is never described in detail from within, because the narrative is confined to Winston Smith's perspective as an Outer Party member of Oceania; he can only know what the Party tells him. This structural silence mirrors the way all three superstates seal their populations from foreign contact, ensuring that the enemy remains a faceless abstraction. The very name "Death-Worship"—with its connotations of nihilism and self-annihilation—serves as a propaganda tool, yet the reader is led to understand that Oceania's own ideology, Ingsoc, is no less destructive of the human spirit.
