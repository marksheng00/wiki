# Doublethink

Doublethink is the central psychological doctrine of Ingsoc, the ideology of Oceania, defined as the capacity to hold two contradictory beliefs at once and accept both as true. It is the mental mechanism that allows the Party to control reality, alter the past, and maintain its absolute power by making the individual complicit in their own deception. For Winston Smith, doublethink is both the concept he first encounters as a disturbing Newspeak word and the system he is ultimately forced to embrace through torture and re-education.

## Definition and Origin

Doublethink is explicitly defined in Emmanuel Goldstein's forbidden book, The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism, as a form of mental discipline whose goal is to believe two contradictory truths simultaneously. Thomas Pynchon's introduction notes that the concept has entered political discourse, describing it as a "schizophrenic manner of thinking" that Orwell observed in the English Left's acceptance of Stalinism as socialism while recognizing its reality. In the novel, Winston Smith first encounters the word when he starts his diary, his mind "fetched up with a bump against the Newspeak word doublethink." The Party's slogan "Who controls the past controls the future: who controls the present controls the past" encapsulates the logic of doublethink, where the past is constantly altered and yet never altered, because the new version becomes the only truth. This process requires "an unending series of victories over your own memory," known as "reality control."

## Mechanism and Operation

The full mental process of doublethink is described during Winston's Physical Jerks: "To know and not to know, to be conscious of complete truthfulness while telling carefully-constructed lies, to hold simultaneously two opinions which cancelled out, knowing them to be contradictory and believing in both of them; to use logic against logic, to repudiate morality while laying claim to it... to forget whatever it was necessary to forget, then to draw it back into memory again at the moment when it was needed, and then promptly to forget it again: and above all, to apply the same process to the process itself." The ultimate subtlety is to consciously induce unconsciousness and then become unconscious of the act of hypnosis. Even understanding the word "doublethink" requires doublethink. This discipline is enforced through the total surveillance of the Thought Police, as described in the book: a Party member lives from birth to death under observation, with no law but only the requirement of correct instincts. The absence of law means that thoughts and actions are not forbidden but punished anyway, forcing Party members to internalize doublethink as a survival mechanism. The Ministry of Truth systematically falsifies records, and the Ministry of Love enforces orthodoxy through torture, but doublethink makes the falsification seamless: the Party member must believe the new version while knowing it is false, and then forget that they ever knew differently.

## Key Instances in the Novel

Winston's first conscious encounter with the term occurs when he writes in his diary, as his mind "fetched up with a bump against the Newspeak word doublethink." Later, during the Physical Jerks, he actively thinks through the labyrinth of doublethink, experiencing its mental contortions. The most dramatic application of doublethink comes during his interrogation by O'Brien, who forces him to accept that he sees five fingers when only four are held up. Under extreme pain, Winston finally screams "Five! Five! Five!" but O'Brien knows he is lying; the goal is not just verbal compliance but genuine belief. Eventually, after the torture in Room 101, Winston achieves a form of doublethink: he writes "2 + 2 = 5" on his slate, accepting that the Party's reality is correct. He also learns to practice "crimestop," the automatic suppression of dangerous thoughts, and trains himself to believe that the Party is infallible. In the end, he loves Big Brother, having fully internalized the doublethink that allows him to hold contradictory feelings of hatred and love simultaneously.

## Thematic Significance

Orwell's concept of doublethink reflects his political analysis of the mid-20th century, particularly the "schizophrenic manner of thinking" he observed among the English Left who accepted Stalin's USSR as socialist while recognizing its brutality. The novel's introduction by Pynchon emphasizes that doublethink is not merely a fictional device but a real phenomenon in modern political discourse, where news media present "balanced" coverage that neutralizes truth, and government agencies like the Ministry of Peace wage war while the Ministry of Love tortures. Doublethink is the psychological foundation of totalitarianism, enabling the Party to deny objective reality, claim that the earth is flat or that ice is heavier than water, and still maintain the appearance of sanity. It also explains the stagnation of science and technology in Oceania: the empirical habit of thought cannot survive in a society where facts are subject to ideological alteration. Ultimately, doublethink represents the most dangerous form of control—not over actions, but over the mind itself, making the victim complicit in their own subjugation.
