# Emmanuel Goldstein

Emmanuel Goldstein is the archetypal villain of the Party in George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four, a figure who never appears in person but whose image dominates the daily Two Minutes Hate and whose name is invoked to explain every deviation. He was once a leading figure of the Party, almost equal to Big Brother, before he was condemned as a traitor and mysteriously disappeared. The Party uses him as the permanent enemy, the source of all heresy, and the commander of a shadowy underground organization called the Brotherhood. Yet for Winston Smith, Goldstein sometimes represents the lonely voice of truth in a world of lies.

## Identity and Appearance

Goldstein is described as a lean Jewish man with a great fuzzy aureole of white hair, a small goatee beard, and a long thin nose with spectacles perched near the end. His face has a sheeplike quality, and his voice echoes that resemblance. According to official Party history, he once stood almost on a level with Big Brother himself before engaging in counter-revolutionary activities, being sentenced to death, and escaping to an unknown location. He is the primal traitor, the earliest defiler of the Party's purity, from whose teaching all subsequent crimes against the Party are said to spring.

## Ideological Role

Goldstein is the indispensable scapegoat of the Party. The Two Minutes Hate always features his face, and his name is the rallying point for collective fury. Although the Party endlessly refutes his theories, his influence never seems to diminish. He is portrayed as the commander of a vast underground network of conspirators, the Brotherhood, dedicated to overthrowing the State. This narrative allows the Party to explain away any resistance as foreign sabotage and to channel popular hatred onto a single safe target. Yet Winston Smith sometimes experiences a contradictory reaction: during the Hate, his heart goes out to the lonely, derided heretic, seeing him as the sole guardian of truth and sanity.

## The Book and the Brotherhood

Goldstein is credited with writing The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism, a forbidden book that circulates clandestinely among Party members. The book provides a systematic analysis of Oceanic society, explaining Ingsoc, doublethink, and the true nature of perpetual war. Winston receives a copy through O'Brien, and it confirms his own inchoate thoughts. The Brotherhood, supposedly led by Goldstein, is a shadowy entity whose existence is never confirmed; Winston is never sure whether it is real or a Party invention. O'Brien later reveals that he himself helped write the book, and that the Brotherhood is a real but elusive conspiracy whose members have no way of recognizing one another.

## Narrative Function and Symbolism

Goldstein is explicitly modeled on Leon Trotsky, just as Big Brother is modeled on Stalin, but Orwell avoids a simple one-to-one allegory. Goldstein's function is structural: he embodies the necessary enemy, the figure of absolute evil against whom the Party defines itself. His absence from the physical action makes him a pure symbol, a construct of Party propaganda. Yet his ideas, as presented in the book, offer the only coherent alternative to the Party's worldview. In the end, even the book's truth is undermined when O'Brien claims authorship, leaving the reader uncertain whether any opposition truly exists outside the Party's narrative. Goldstein's name is the last word Winston hears before his final conversion, linking the hated figure to the instrument of his destruction.
