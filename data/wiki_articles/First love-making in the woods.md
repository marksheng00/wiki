# First love-making in the woods

On a May afternoon in the Golden Country—a landscape that had haunted Winston Smith's dreams—he and Julia met for the first time as lovers. The encounter was not merely a private consummation but a calculated political act, a blow struck against the Party's totalitarian control over desire. Julia, a member of the Junior Anti-Sex League in appearance, revealed herself as a practiced rebel who had long understood that the regime's sexual puritanism was a tool to channel energy into war-fever and leader-worship. For Winston, the act embodied the hope that the Party might be rotten from within, that simple undifferentiated lust could tear its edifice apart.

## The Golden Country and the Approach

Julia led Winston through a leafy lane to a hidden clearing she had discovered on a community hike. The place matched the dream landscape he called the Golden Country: an old rabbit-bitten pasture, a stream with green pools where dace swam, and a thrush that sang into the afternoon hush. She had scouted the area thoroughly, knowing where microphones could be hidden and where they could speak without fear. When they reached the ring of saplings, she turned to him with a faint, bold smile. The gesture with which she tore off her clothes seemed to annihilate a whole culture, a whole system of thought—as though Big Brother and the Party could be swept away by a single movement of the arm.

## The Exchange of Confessions

Kneeling face to face, Winston asked if she had done this before. She answered casually: 'Hundreds of times—well, scores of times, anyway.' All with Party members, never with the Inner Party, whom she called swine. Winston's heart leaped at the revelation. He told her that the more men she had had, the more he loved her. 'I hate purity, I hate goodness! I don't want any virtue to exist anywhere. I want everyone to be corrupt to the bones.' Julia responded with perfect understanding: 'I'm corrupt to the bones.' She confessed that she adored the act itself, not merely him. For Winston, this was the confirmation he craved: not the love of one person, but the animal instinct, the simple undifferentiated desire that would tear the Party to pieces.

## The Act and Its Meaning

He pressed her down upon the grass among the fallen bluebells. This time there was no difficulty. They made love with a sense of victory, and afterward fell asleep in the sun. When Winston woke, he watched Julia's peaceful face and studied her body. The mindless tenderness he had felt earlier was gone, replaced by a recognition that their embrace had been a battle, the climax a victory. 'No emotion was pure,' he thought, 'because everything was mixed up with fear and hatred. Their embrace had been a blow struck against the Party. It was a political act.'

## Aftermath and Renewal

Julia woke with her characteristic practicality. She immediately began arranging the details of the journey home, insisting on different routes and caution. She told him they could use the hide-out again but only after a month or two. She had a deep well of cunning and experience, and Winston felt a protective tenderness toward her. Yet he also saw that she did not fully grasp the inevitable doom that awaited them. She believed in the possibility of constructing a secret world, while he knew that from the moment of declaring war on the Party, it was better to think of oneself as a corpse. 'We are the dead,' he said. 'We're not dead yet,' she replied prosaically.
