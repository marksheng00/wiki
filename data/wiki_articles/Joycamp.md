# Joycamp

Joycamp is a Newspeak word for a forced-labour camp, and one of the clearest examples in the invented language of Oceania of a term that means almost the exact opposite of what it appears to say. It belongs to the B vocabulary of words deliberately constructed for political purposes, and although the camps it names are places of punishment, deprivation and slave labour, the sound of the word carries the associations of a holiday. In the Newspeak system no word of the B vocabulary is ideologically neutral, and Joycamp is designed to shape the speaker's attitude before any fact about the camp itself is mentioned.

## A Euphemism Built into the Language

In the B vocabulary, euphemism and frankness operate side by side. Words like Joycamp, or the name of the Ministry of Peace for the institution that actually wages war, reverse the visible meaning of things, while a word like Prolefeed frankly despises the rubbishy entertainment and spurious news the Party hands to the masses. Joycamp belongs to the first group. Its purpose is not merely to name an institution but to make the horror of it easier to carry: forced labour, starvation and death are covered with the vocabulary of recreation and contentment.

## The Society Behind the Word

The society in which Joycamp functions is one where concealment is continuous. An involuntary expression can be Facecrime, a nervous tic, an unconscious look of anxiety or a habit of muttering to yourself can give you away, and an improper expression is itself a punishable offence. Winston Smith, leaving the Ministry at lunchtime with nothing but dark bread saved for tomorrow, gulps a teacupful of Victory Gin like a dose of medicine while the telescreen watches him; he wears, when facing the screen, the expression of quiet optimism which it is advisable to wear. Julia has made the same discipline into a strategy of survival: a former troop-leader in the Spies and a tireless volunteer for the Junior Anti-Sex League, she pastes up posters, carries banners and yells with the crowd because it is the only way to be safe. The cheerful name of the camps is the reverse side of that performance; it is the punishment held in reserve for anyone whose mask slips.

## Significance

Joycamp matters because it shows how Newspeak works as an instrument of rule. The language does not simply describe Oceania; it manufactures a second reality in which torture is named as joy and the Ministry of Peace wages war. A term like Joycamp makes the true nature of punishment harder to put into words, and so helps to make thoughtcrime itself harder to conceive. In the same way that Newspeak aims to narrow the range of thought until heretical ideas are literally unthinkable, the cheerful vocabulary of the camps makes their terror unsayable, and therefore less real.
