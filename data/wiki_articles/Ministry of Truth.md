# Ministry of Truth

The Ministry of Truth rises three hundred metres above the grimy landscape of London, a glittering white pyramidal structure that dwarfs the rotting nineteenth-century houses and bombed sites around it. From its white face, the three slogans of the Party — WAR IS PEACE, FREEDOM IS SLAVERY, IGNORANCE IS STRENGTH — are picked out in elegant lettering. It is one of four identical ministries that divide the entire apparatus of government, the others being the Ministry of Peace (Minipax), the Ministry of Love (Miniluv), and the Ministry of Plenty (Miniplenty). The Ministry of Truth is said to contain three thousand rooms above ground and corresponding ramifications below, and its sheer scale makes it visible from the roof of Victory Mansions, where Winston Smith lives.

## Architecture and Location

The Ministry of Truth is startlingly different from any other object in sight, an enormous pyramidal structure of glittering white concrete soaring up terrace after terrace. From the window of his flat, Winston can see it a kilometre away, towering vast and white above the grimy landscape. The building's three thousand rooms above ground level and corresponding subterranean levels house the entire apparatus of news, entertainment, education, and the fine arts. Its four ministries — Minitrue, Minipax, Miniluv, and Miniplenty — are the only buildings of similar size in London, so completely do they dwarf the surrounding architecture.

## Functions and Daily Operations

The Ministry of Truth's primary job is not to reconstruct the past but to supply the citizens of Oceania with newspapers, films, textbooks, telescreen programmes, plays, novels — every conceivable kind of information, instruction, or entertainment. Within its labyrinthine corridors, the Records Department, where Winston works, is a single cell in a vast network. Winston's daily routine involves receiving messages through a pneumatic tube, dictating corrections into a speakwrite, and dropping original documents into memory holes that lead to hidden furnaces. The work is a continuous process of falsification: rewriting speeches, altering statistics, and fabricating events to make the Party's predictions always correct. Statistics are often made up out of thin air, as when Winston adjusts the boot production forecast from a fantasy figure to another fantasy figure, knowing that the real output is unknowable.

## Power and Ideology

The Ministry of Truth embodies the Party's absolute control over reality. A Party member lives from birth to death under the eye of the Thought Police, with no law to protect him — only the certainty that any deviation will be detected and punished. The aim of the Party is not merely to control actions but to remove all pleasure from the sexual act, to kill the sex instinct, and to ensure that all marriages are approved by a committee. The bareness, dinginess, and listlessness of modern life are the result of this systematic suppression. Yet the proles, left to themselves, have remained human, loyal to one another rather than to the Party, and it is in them that Winston places his hope.

## Narrative Significance

The Ministry of Truth is the setting for Winston's daily rebellion and his ultimate despair. It is where he commits thoughtcrime by beginning a diary, where he works beside Syme and Parsons, and where he receives the love note from Julia. The building's pyramidal shape, with its glittering white concrete and elegant slogans, becomes a symbol of the Party's impregnable power. Winston's heart quails before it, knowing that a thousand rocket bombs could not batter it down. The Ministry of Truth is not merely a workplace; it is the physical manifestation of the Party's ability to control the past, and thus the future, and the place where Winston's hope of finding evidence against the Party is ultimately destroyed.
