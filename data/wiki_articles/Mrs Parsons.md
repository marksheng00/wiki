# Mrs Parsons

Mrs Parsons is a neighbour of Winston Smith in Victory Mansions, a woman whose worn appearance and helpless manner epitomise the domestic degradation that pervades life under the Party. Her immediate plight—a blocked kitchen sink, her husband's absence, and a face lined with dust—reveals a daily existence stripped of comfort, competence, and privacy, even for a Party member.

## Home Life and Domestic Decay

When Winston answers her knock, Mrs Parsons greets him in a dreary, whining voice and immediately asks him to look at her blocked kitchen sink. She explains that she would not have troubled him if her husband, Tom, were home, but Parsons is still at work. The flat itself is a chaos of children's games, dirty dishes, and the pervasive reek of sweat, and the sink is full of foul greenish water. The scene underscores the squalor of Victory Mansions, where even basic repairs are an almost daily irritation that falls to neighbours like Winston.

In the living room, Mrs Parsons's eyes flit nervously between Winston and her children, who have just menaced him with a toy pistol and a catapult. In the better light, Winston notices that there is actually dust in the creases of her face. Her fearful, helpless look suggests a life lived in constant tension, afraid of her own children—who are being trained by the Spies to denounce any deviation—and unable to escape the drudgery and dread that the Party's system imposes on ordinary families.
