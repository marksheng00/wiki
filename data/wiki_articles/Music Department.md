# Music Department

The Music Department is a sub-section of the Ministry of Truth in Oceania, charged with producing entertainment for the proles. Its most visible output is a stream of sentimental songs, such as the tune ‘It was only a hopeless fancy,’ which had been haunting London for weeks. The lyrics were composed without any human intervention by an instrument known as a versificator, and the songs were published ‘for the benefit of the proles.’

## Function and Methods

The Music Department operates as a branch of the Ministry’s larger effort to supply the proletariat with rubbishy newspapers, sensational five-cent novelettes, and sentimental songs. The songs are mechanically generated by a versificator, a kaleidoscope-like device that produces lyrics without human input. The prole woman in the courtyard sings the song tunefully, turning the dreadful rubbish into an almost pleasant sound, but the content is designed to be trivial and politically neutral.

## Cultural and Political Significance

The Music Department’s output reflects the Party’s strategy of keeping the proles amused and politically inert. The songs are ‘drivelling’ and ‘dreadful rubbish’ by Winston Smith’s standards, yet they are sung with genuine feeling by the proles. Orwell, as Pynchon notes, had a personal fondness for popular culture and may have written the melody himself, but within the novel the Music Department represents the mechanical, cynical production of mass entertainment as a tool of social control.
