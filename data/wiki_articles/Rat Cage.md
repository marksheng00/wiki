# Rat Cage

The Rat Cage is a torture device employed in Room 101 of the Ministry of Love, designed to break a prisoner's resistance by confronting him with his worst fear. It is an oblong wire cage with a handle on top and a fencing-mask attachment fixed to the front, divided lengthwise into two compartments each containing a rat. The cage is constructed to fit over the victim's head, with the mask covering the face completely, leaving no exit. When the lever is pressed, the door slides up, allowing the starving rats to leap onto the victim's face and burrow into it. O'Brien uses this device against Winston Smith, who has a phobia of rats, to force him to betray Julia. The cage represents the Party's ultimate tool of psychological coercion, exploiting individual vulnerabilities that cannot be overcome by courage or willpower. As O'Brien explains, for everyone there is something unendurable, and the cage is tailored to that specific weakness.

## Description and Role in the Narrative

The cage is described as an oblong wire cage with a handle on top, and a fencing-mask-like attachment with the concave side outward. It is divided lengthwise into two compartments, each holding a rat. The mask is designed to fit over the victim's head, ensuring that the rats will attack the face. The cage is portable, carried by a handle, and is brought into Room 101 by a guard. In the narrative, the cage is the centerpiece of Winston's final test. O'Brien, aware of Winston's terror of rats, uses the cage to force him to choose between his own suffering and betraying Julia. When the cage is brought close, Winston succumbs and shouts "Do it to Julia!" This moment completes his psychological breakdown, demonstrating that the Party can destroy even the deepest personal loyalties. The cage thus symbolizes the Party's power to control the individual through the exploitation of primal fears, a power that surpasses physical torture and reaches into the core of the self.
