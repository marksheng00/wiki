# Sanity is statistical

"Sanity is statistical" is an implicit doctrine of the Party in Oceania, which equates truth with the beliefs of the collective—or more precisely, with whatever the Party declares the collective to believe. Winston Smith rejects this doctrine after reading a portion of Emmanuel Goldstein's forbidden book, when he reflects that "being in a minority, even a minority of one, did not make you mad." Drifting off to sleep in the room above Mr Charrington's shop, with the sun on his face and Julia's body beside him, he murmurs the phrase "Sanity is not statistical," feeling that it contains profound wisdom. This moment crystallizes his faith in objective reality and his refusal to surrender to the Party's control over truth.

## The Refusal of Statistical Sanity

The context of Winston's assertion is crucial. He has just read the first chapter of The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism, which systematizes his own understanding of the Party's methods. The knowledge that he is not alone in his thinking—even if physically alone—reassures him that his sanity is intact. The Party's logic, as Winston understands it, would hold that if everyone believes a lie, the lie becomes truth, and anyone who dissents must be insane. Winston counters this by insisting that truth is not a function of consensus. The physical details of the scene—the sinking sun on his face, Julia's body beside him, the sense of safety—underline the emotional power of this conviction. Yet the assertion "Sanity is not statistical" is uttered in a whisper, as a private mantra, suggesting how fragile and isolated such a belief must remain in Oceania.
