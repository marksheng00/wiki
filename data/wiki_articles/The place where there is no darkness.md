# The place where there is no darkness

The place where there is no darkness is the cryptic phrase spoken to Winston Smith by O'Brien in a dream, seven years before the novel opens; it becomes the promise and finally the name of their meeting place in the Ministry of Love. More than a password, it is a thread of imagined understanding that lets Winston believe O'Brien is on his side, and its fulfilment reveals that the understanding was real only in the way a trap is real.

## Origin in a Dream

Seven years before the story's present, Winston dreamed that he was walking through a pitch-dark room, and someone sitting to one side of him said as he passed, "We shall meet in the place where there is no darkness." It was said very quietly, almost casually, a statement rather than a command, and he walked on without pausing. At the time the words made little impression on him; only later, by degrees, did they seem to take on significance. He could not remember whether he had seen O'Brien before or after the dream, nor when he first identified the voice as O'Brien's, but the identification existed: it was O'Brien who had spoken to him out of the dark.

## Recurrence and Interpretation

After the Two Minutes Hate, Winston could never be sure whether O'Brien was a friend or an enemy, and yet there was a link of understanding between them that mattered more than affection or partisanship. The sentence returned to him as a pledge: Winston did not know what it meant, only that in some way or another it would come true. Later, trying to summon O'Brien's image in his flat, he thought he knew its meaning: the place where there is no darkness was the imagined future, which one would never see but which, by foreknowledge, one could mystically share in. When he and Julia finally came to O'Brien's flat and enrolled themselves in the Brotherhood, Winston asked "In the place where there is no darkness?" and O'Brien nodded without surprise, repeating the words as though he had recognized the allusion all along.

## Fulfilment in the Ministry of Love

In the windowless cell of the Ministry of Love, where the lights were never turned out, Winston understood the allusion at last: "It was the place with no darkness: he saw now why O'Brien had seemed to recognise the allusion." The promised meeting place was not the future but the interrogation cell, where there is no night, no hiding, and no unobserved thought. During the long confusion of interrogation a voice murmured in his ear; Winston could not remember whether it came in drugged sleep, normal sleep, or a moment of wakefulness, but it was the same voice that had said, "We shall meet in the place where there is no darkness," in that other dream, seven years ago.

## Thematic Significance

Darkness is Winston's only privacy: he can think freely only in the dark, beyond the telescreen's eye. The place where there is no darkness therefore names the exact point where private consciousness ceases to exist — the Ministry of Love, where the Party finally gets inside its victims and where O'Brien, as tormentor and protector, keeps his word. The phrase's tenderness is the trap's most sinister instrument: it makes betrayal feel like a reunion.
