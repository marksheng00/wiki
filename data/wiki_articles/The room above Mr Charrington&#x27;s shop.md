# The room above Mr Charrington's shop

The room above Mr Charrington's shop is a shabby, time-worn space in the prole district of London, rented by Winston Smith as a clandestine refuge for his affair with Julia. It is a small chamber reached by a steep, worn staircase, furnished with a strip of carpet, a few pictures, a deep slatternly armchair, and an old-fashioned glass clock with a twelve-hour face on the mantelpiece. The room's most prominent feature is an enormous bed with ragged blankets and a coverless bolster, occupying nearly a quarter of the floor. On a gate-leg table in the corner lies the glass paperweight Winston bought from the shop, gleaming softly in the half-darkness. The room looks out onto a cobbled yard and a forest of chimney pots, and its most remarkable absence is a telescreen: the surveillance device that dominates every Party member's home is not present here, making the space feel curiously silent and private.

## Sanctuary and Symbol of the Past

For Winston and Julia, the room is a paradise, a place where they can be alone and physically intimate without the omnipresent watch of the Party. The room's lack of a telescreen allows them to speak freely and even to undress without fear of being seen. Here they make love on the double bed, a luxury nearly unknown among Party members, and they share black-market food such as real coffee, sugar, and white bread smuggled by Julia. The room becomes a world unto itself, a pocket of the past where extinct things can still exist. Winston gazes into the glass paperweight and feels that the room itself is inside the crystal, fixed in eternity, and that time might be arrested. The oldfashioned clock, the steel engraving of St Clement's Dane, and the scraps of forgotten nursery rhymes that Mr Charrington recites all reinforce the sense that this room preserves a vanished era before the Party's rewriting of history.

## The End of the Sanctuary

The room's fragile sanctuary is shattered when the Thought Police burst in during Winston and Julia's final meeting. The telescreen hidden behind the picture frame reveals that Mr Charrington is a member of the Thought Police. The glass paperweight is smashed to pieces on the hearthstone, and the coral fragment within rolls across the mat. Julia is beaten and dragged away, and Winston is arrested. The room, which had offered a brief illusion of safety and permanence, is revealed as a trap, and its destruction marks the end of any hope of escape.
