# Victory Cigarettes

Victory Cigarettes are the brand of cigarettes Winston Smith smokes in the world of Nineteen Eighty-Four: cheap, loosely packed, and marked with the Party's favourite word for official optimism. Their appearances in the novel are brief but telling. A crumpled packet surfaces just as Winston is about to open the diary that will condemn him, and the cigarette he takes from it spills its tobacco on the floor when he holds it upright. That failure, repeated whenever he tries to smoke, turns a trivial habit into a running emblem of Oceania's material collapse: a state that promises victory cannot even keep a cigarette together.

## A Cigarette That Will Not Stay Together

Winston's first cigarette in the novel is part of a chain of small physical indignities. After nerving himself to swallow a teacupful of Victory Gin, he takes a cigarette from a crumpled packet marked VICTORY CIGARETTES and incautiously holds it upright, whereupon the tobacco falls out onto the floor. The second cigarette is more successful, and he carries it to the small table beside the telescreen, where the diary waits. The gesture is never developed, but it establishes a pattern: in later scenes Winston is careful to hold his cigarette horizontal, because the tobacco will not stay in the paper if it is standing up. A cigarette that requires constant attention is a minuscule act of labour in an economy where nothing works.

## Scarcity and the Discipline of Survival

In the crowded Ministry canteen the cigarette becomes a measure of how far a man must stretch his resources. Winston is smoking a Victory Cigarette held carefully horizontal and knows he has only four cigarettes left before the new ration begins tomorrow. When another cigarette goes out in the canteen, he lays it on the edge of the table, intending to finish it after work if he can keep the tobacco in it, even while reflecting that he may be taken to the cellars of the Ministry of Love within three days. The whistle that sends everyone back to work makes the remaining tobacco fall out anyway. The detail belongs to a world in which a half-smoked cigarette end cannot be wasted, however great the danger around it.

## Comfort in the Enemy's Hands

The only good cigarettes in the novel belong to O'Brien. When Winston and Julia are admitted to O'Brien's softly lit flat, he pushes across a silver box of cigarettes that are thick, well packed and smooth in the paper, qualities Winston's own cigarettes never possess. Julia, usually alert to the dangers of the scene, lets her cigarette go out as she listens to O'Brien. The contrast is not incidental: the Inner Party lives on a different material plane from the Outer Party, and the cigarette that stays together is part of the privilege that the crumbling Victory Cigarette denies.

## Narrative Significance

The crumbling cigarette is one of the recurring objects through which the novel shows the cost of Party rule in the textures of ordinary life. Winston's tobacco falls out at the moment he tries to write his first diary entry, again in the crowded canteen, and again late at night when he tries to summon the thought of O'Brien and instead tastes bitter tobacco dust on his tongue. The repeated failure makes the object obtrusive exactly when private feeling is at stake, and the brand name becomes part of the novel's larger irony: 'Victory' is the same word that labels the burning gin, the decaying apartment block, and the state's claims about itself. A cigarette that will not stay intact is a tiny, daily refutation of everything the Party says.
