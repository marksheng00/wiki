# two-way television

The two-way television, or telescreen, is the central surveillance and propaganda device of the Party in Oceania. It is an oblong metal plaque, like a dulled mirror, set into the wall of every Party member's living space. It can be dimmed but never completely shut off, and it simultaneously receives and transmits sound and vision: any sound above a very low whisper is picked up, and any movement within its field of vision is scrutinized. The Thought Police can plug in at any moment, and citizens must live under the constant assumption that they are being watched and heard.

## Physical Operation and Design

The telescreen is a permanent fixture in the walls of Party members' flats, embedded as part of the surface. In Winston Smith's flat, it is located in the living room, and unusually placed in the longer wall opposite the window, allowing a shallow alcove where one can sit outside its direct line of sight. The device can be dimmed to lower the volume, but there is no way to switch it off entirely. It receives and transmits simultaneously, so that any sound above a whisper is detected, and anyone within its field of vision is visible. The system is such that there is no way to know at any given moment whether one is being watched, but the possibility is ever-present.

## Surveillance and Psychological Control

The telescreen's bidirectional nature enforces a state of perpetual self-discipline. Citizens must assume that every sound they make is overheard and every movement scrutinized, except in total darkness. The uncertainty of surveillance—whether the Thought Police are listening at any given moment—is more powerful than overt force, as it compels internalized obedience. This mechanism creates a world where privacy is abolished and the Party's power is inscribed into the very fabric of daily life.

## Role in Daily Life

The telescreen structures the day from morning to night. It broadcasts the ear-splitting whistle that signals waking time, followed by the synchronized Physical Jerks exercises with a female instructor barking commands. Throughout the day it pours out a constant stream of propaganda, production statistics, military music, and news. It is the source of the Two Minutes Hate and all official announcements. Even at night, the telescreen is only dimmed, never silenced, ensuring that the Party's voice and gaze are always present.
